Orchestration, steerability, and the craft of designing when an AI agent is part of the experience.

I'm Pratik Tamgadge, a UX Lead designing for a shifting frontier: products where an AI agent is part of the experience, not just a backend feature. My focus is orchestration — how steps and tools hand off cleanly — and steerability — how a person can redirect, correct, or override a system without losing trust in it. I'm currently working remotely from Pune with Testlio, with a client portfolio spanning Snapchat, Microsoft (SharePoint/WEX), Bet365, Bajaj Motors, and PlugShare, and earlier roles at Expleo Group and Paperlite.io.

Underneath that practice sits years of quality engineering — not as a separate career track, but as the actual source of the instinct. Designing with a QA background means I don't just design the ideal path; I design for what happens when the decision is uncertain, when a fallback state triggers, when trust has to be rebuilt after something goes wrong. Most portfolios show you the polished flow. Mine tends to show you the flow and the edge case it almost missed — because that's genuinely what I deliver.

That perspective took shape gradually. Early roles at Paperlite.io and Expleo Group put me on both sides of the design/QA line before I framed it as a deliberate UX practice rather than two separate skill sets. Since then, the work has ranged from iGaming platforms balancing engagement against player wellbeing, to enterprise tools like Microsoft SharePoint/WEX, to consumer products at Snapchat's scale — each one asking for a different balance of craft and rigor, and increasingly, a different question about how much a system should decide on its own.

How I work

I build with AI as a core part of my process — which is also where the orchestration instinct gets tested firsthand. Claude handles product thinking and content — working through user flows, edge cases, and copy before a single screen gets built. Cursor turns that thinking into working implementation. Figma Make is where I move fast on visual exploration once direction is clear. Replit gets things deployed and in front of real users quickly, rather than staying a polished mockup forever. Mareflo, my independent gaming social platform, is a direct product of that workflow — see the case study.

When I'm not designing, I'm usually building PCs or deep in competitive gaming — which, more than once, has directly informed how I think about player experience and responsible design in gaming products. Understanding why a UI decision frustrates a player at 2am mid-session is a different kind of knowledge than reading about it in a research report, and it shows up directly in how I approach engagement and responsible-design work like the iGaming case study.
