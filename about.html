UX Lead & QA Engineer —
two disciplines, one practice.

Most designers hand off to QA. I've spent 11+ years doing both.

I'm Pratik Tamgadge, a UX Lead and QA Engineer with 11+ years of experience, currently working remotely from Pune with Testlio. My client portfolio spans Snapchat, Microsoft (SharePoint/WEX), Bet365, Bajaj Motors, and PlugShare, with earlier roles at Expleo Group and Paperlite.io.

The dual identity isn't a hedge between two career paths — it's the actual differentiator. Designing with a QA instinct means I catch the edge cases before a reviewer has to. Testing with a designer's eye means I flag what's technically correct but still feels wrong to use. Most portfolios show you the polished flow. Mine tends to show you the flow and the edge case it almost missed — because that's genuinely how I work.

That perspective took shape gradually. Early roles at Paperlite.io and Expleo Group put me on both sides of the design/QA line before I framed it as a deliberate practice rather than two separate skill sets. Since then, the work has ranged from iGaming platforms balancing engagement against player wellbeing, to enterprise tools like Microsoft SharePoint/WEX, to consumer products at Snapchat's scale — each one asking for a different balance of craft and rigor.

How I work

I build with AI as a core part of my process, not an add-on. Claude handles product thinking and content — working through user flows, edge cases, and copy before a single screen gets built. Cursor turns that thinking into working implementation. Figma Make is where I move fast on visual exploration once direction is clear. Replit gets things deployed and in front of real users quickly, rather than staying a polished mockup forever. Mareflo, my independent gaming social platform, is a direct product of that workflow — see the case study.

When I'm not designing, I'm usually building PCs or deep in competitive gaming — which, more than once, has directly informed how I think about player experience and responsible design in gaming products. Understanding why a UI decision frustrates a player at 2am mid-session is a different kind of knowledge than reading about it in a research report, and it shows up directly in how I approach engagement and responsible-design work like the iGaming case study.
