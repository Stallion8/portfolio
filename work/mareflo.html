Rethinking how gamers react.

An independent gaming social platform MVP, designed, prototyped, and built solo — anchored by an original interaction pattern called MoodDial.

01 — Context

Gaming social is broken — and binary reactions don't help.

Mareflo started from a frustration I kept running into as a player and designer: mainstream social platforms weren't built for gaming culture. Clips get buried, banter gets flagged as harassment, and a single like can't distinguish a clutch win from a lucky one.

The core problem isn't just toxicity — it's context blindness. Platforms punish intent they can't read. Mareflo's bet: understand emotional context instead of flattening everything into engagement metrics.

Traditional social

  • Likes measure popularity, not feeling
  • Keyword moderation misses gaming banter
  • Gaming content buried in general feeds
  • Algorithms optimize outrage and addiction

Mareflo approach

  • MoodDial captures emotional nuance
  • Context-aware moderation (friends vs strangers)
  • Built for gamers and virtual photographers
  • Quality-of-resonance over vanity metrics
Gaming banter: "Get rekt noob! GG EZ!" between friends → playful, not harassment.
Constructive critique: "This game is disappointing" → downbeat feedback, not hidden negativity.
Shared struggle: A Dark Souls death → downbeat solidarity, not a confusing heart icon.
02 — Interaction design

MoodDial — nuance without friction.

So I designed MoodDial — an interaction pattern that replaces the single-tap reaction with dual emoji sliders. A player expresses what they felt and how much in one gesture — fast enough to happen in the two seconds after a clip ends.

The portfolio demo below uses a simplified slider model. In the full product, MoodDial expands into upbeat and downbeat emotional contexts — each with weighted emoji reactions that feed creator analytics and feed ranking differently than a like count ever could.

03 — Platform thinking

Context over content — the system around MoodDial.

MoodDial is the signature interaction, but Mareflo is a full platform thesis: emotionally intelligent social design for gamers. These are the supporting systems I designed and built around the reaction moment.

Context-aware moderation

Relationship-aware AI distinguishes friends trash-talking from strangers harassing — gaming slang like "GG EZ" protected when context supports it.

Vibe compatibility

Follow graph weighted by emotional alignment, not follower count — surfacing people whose reactions and play style actually match yours.

Emotional metrics

Creators see why content resonated — fire, laughter, heartbreak — not just that it got engagement. Actionable insight instead of a vanity number.

Principle What it means in practice
Context over content Same words, different relationships → different moderation and feed treatment
Emotion over engagement Rank and surface by resonance depth, not raw like velocity
Community over clout Vibe match and mood balance over follower status
Adaptation over assumption UI and themes respond to emotional state, not one static chrome
04 — Build

Solo, AI-augmented, shipped.

I built the entire MVP myself using an AI-augmented workflow: Claude for product thinking and content, Cursor for implementation, Figma Make for rapid prototyping, and Replit for deployment — going from concept to a working product without a traditional dev team.

In practice that meant thinking through user flows and edge cases with Claude before touching a screen, exploring the MoodDial's visual behavior fast in Figma Make once the interaction logic was solid, implementing it properly in Cursor, and shipping to a live, testable build in Replit — compressing a process that would normally involve a small team into one continuous loop.
